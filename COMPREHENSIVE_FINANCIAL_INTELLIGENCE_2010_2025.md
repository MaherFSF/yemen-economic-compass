## Critical Findings Summary

### Banking Sector Fragmentation (2016-2025)
The 2016 Central Bank of Yemen split created two parallel banking systems, forcing all institutions to navigate dual regulatory frameworks. This fragmentation has been the single most significant challenge facing the sector, leading to:
- Operational splits (banks maintaining presence in both Aden and Sana'a)
- Regulatory arbitrage opportunities
- Compliance challenges with international standards
- Liquidity crises and foreign currency shortages

### OFAC Sanctions (January 2025)
Three major Yemeni banks were designated by the U.S. Treasury:
1. **Yemen Kuwait Bank** - Supporting Houthis, money laundering
2. **International Bank of Yemen** - Facilitating Houthi finances
3. **Saba Islamic Bank** - Aiding Iranian oil sales

These sanctions have severely restricted these banks' access to the international financial system and triggered CBY-Aden's Circular #4/2025 mandating enhanced due diligence.

### Microfinance Resilience
Despite the conflict, several microfinance institutions demonstrated remarkable resilience:
- **Al-Kuraimi Islamic Microfinance** - 50% branch expansion
- **Al-Amal Microfinance** - 20% growth
- **Tadhamon Islamic Bank** - 54% branch network expansion

This growth highlights the critical role of microfinance in sustaining economic activity during crisis.

### Correspondent Banking De-risking
International banks have progressively withdrawn correspondent banking relationships with Yemeni banks due to:
- Sanctions risk
- AML/CFT compliance concerns
- Reputational risk
- Regulatory pressure

This has severely constrained international transfers and trade finance.
